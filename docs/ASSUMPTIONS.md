# Modelling assumptions

Every assumption the math relies on, with its reason and a reference. Approved by the maintainer on 2026-11-12 (Phase R gate). Derivations: `docs/research/math-review.md`.

| # | Assumption | Reason | Reference |
|---|---|---|---|
| A1 | An asymmetric tolerance is re-centred on its midpoint: `μ = nominal + (U+L)/2`, `t = (U−L)/2`. The process is assumed centred on that midpoint, not on the nominal. | Gives an equal-bilateral tolerance, which every stack formula needs. A process that targets the nominal instead is not modelled. | Algebra; Scholz (1995) §2 notes asymmetric intervals are rarely treated in the literature. |
| A2 | Normal contributor: `σ = t/(3·cp)`. | Follows from `Cp = (USL − LSL)/(6σ)` with `USL − LSL = 2t`. cp = 1 means 0.27 % of parts are out of tolerance. | NIST/SEMATECH e-Handbook §6.1.6. https://www.itl.nist.gov/div898/handbook/pmc/section1/pmc16.htm |
| A3 | Uniform contributor over `[μ−t, μ+t]`: `σ = t/√3`. | Variance of a uniform distribution is (width)²/12. | Definition; Scholz (1995) Table 1 (factor 1.732). |
| A4 | Truncated-normal contributor: underlying normal with `σ0 = t/(3·cp)`, truncated at `μ ± t`; `σ = σ0·√(1 − 2kφ(k)/(2Φ(k) − 1))`, `k = 3·cp`. | Models parts that are inspected and rejected outside tolerance. k = 3 → 0.986578·σ0. | Derived in math-review.md item 5; verified by reviewer subagent. |
| A5 | Contributors are statistically independent. | Required for `σ_Y² = Σ aᵢ²σᵢ²`. Parts from one lot may be correlated; that makes RSS optimistic. | Scholz (1995) §4.1. |
| A6 | Analytic P_fail, Cpk/Cpu/Cpl and "μ_Y ± 3σ_Y = 99.73 %" assume Y is normal. Exact only for linear stacks with all-normal inputs. | Central limit theorem; fails with few contributors, one dominant contributor, or non-normal inputs. | Scholz (1995) §4.2; NIST e-Handbook §6.1.6. |
| A7 | Worst case uses the tolerance limits `μ_Y ± Σ|aᵢ|tᵢ`. Samples from an unbounded `normal` contributor can fall outside it in Monte Carlo. | Worst case is a statement about tolerance limits, not about the sampling distribution. | Scholz (1995) §3. |
| A8 | Monte Carlo failure fraction: Wilson interval, two-sided 95 % (z = 1.959964). With zero failures: exact one-sided 95 % upper bound `1 − 0.05^(1/N)` (≈ 3/N). | The two bounds are different confidence statements and are labelled as such. | NIST e-Handbook §7.2.4.1; Hanley & Lippman-Hand, JAMA 249:1743 (1983). |
| A9 | The Monte Carlo convergence check (std at N/2 vs N, warn above 1 %) is a heuristic, not a proof of convergence. It does not test the failure fraction; the Wilson interval does. | No authoritative source found for the criterion; at N = 100 000 the threshold is about 4.5 standard deviations. | math-review.md item 19. |
| A10 | Modified (Bender) RSS: `half-range = k·√(Σ(aᵢtᵢ)²)`; uses `tᵢ` directly and ignores cp and distribution. | Empirical inflation factor, k = 1.5. Can exceed worst case for few contributors; a warning is reported. | Bender (1962) as cited by Scholz (1995) §4.2. Primary not read. |
| A11 | Units are millimetres and radians internally. Finite-difference step `h = max(1e-6, 1e-4·t)` is in the contributor's own unit. | Spec rule; step is near the unscaled optimum ε^(1/3) for central differences. | root.md §0, §3.4; math-review.md item 23. |

## Vector loops (Phase 3)

Derivations and reviewer check: `docs/reports/phase-3.md`. Status of each convention: `docs/DECISIONS.md` D22–D30.

| # | Assumption | Reason | Reference |
|---|---|---|---|
| A12 | Angles in a vector loop are **relative**: φ_j is the counter-clockwise angle from the previous vector's direction to vector j; φ_1 is measured from the global +x axis. Absolute direction Θ_j = φ_1 + … + φ_j. A change in one angle therefore rotates that vector and every vector after it. | Maintainer's choice (relative angles). Matches the usual vector-loop formulation. An angle toleranced from a datum on the drawing (absolute) must **not** be entered as relative: the sensitivities differ in sign and size (bracket test: +4.64 vs −30 mm/rad). | Maintainer decision 2026-11-12; Gao, Chase & Magleby (1998), abstract only. |
| A13 | The requirement is the projection of the open chain's resultant on a fixed measurement direction α, plus a constant: `Y = constant + Σ L_j cos(Θ_j − α)`. Positive Y points along α. α and the constant are exact (not toleranced). | Simplest sign rule that reproduces a 1D stack: vectors along +α add, vectors against it subtract. | Derived; `tests/core/test_loop.py`. |
| A14 | Worst case, RSS, contributions, P_fail and Cpk of a loop are **linearised**: sensitivities `a_i = ∂Y/∂x_i` at the means, `mean_Y = Y(μ)`. The second-order mean shift is ignored, the linear worst case is not a true bound, and a variable whose sensitivity is zero at the mean contributes nothing. Monte Carlo uses the exact Y. | Spec §3.4. The tool reports `ZERO_SENSITIVITY` and `LINEARISATION_SUSPECT` when the linear model is visibly wrong. | Spec §3.4; math-review.md item 22. |
| A15 | Lengths are millimetres and angles radians. An angle contributor's nominal, tolerances and sigma are in radians and its sensitivity in mm/rad. All variables are independent. Only open 2D chains: no closed loops, no kinematic adjustment between mating parts, no 3D. | Spec rule 5. Scope kept to what the Phase 3 closed-form tests cover. | root.md §0, Phase 3. |

## 3D vector loops

| # | Assumption | Reason | Reference |
|---|---|---|---|
| A16 | In a 3D loop every vector has three **relative** angles. The frame of vector j is the previous frame rotated by yaw ψ (about z), then pitch β (about the new y), then roll ρ (about the vector's own axis): `R_j = R_{j−1}·Rz(ψ)·Ry(β)·Rx(ρ)`, right-handed. The vector points along local x. For a single vector: direction = (cos ψ cos β, sin ψ cos β, −sin β), so **positive pitch tilts towards −z**. Roll never moves its own vector, only later ones. `Y = constant + n̂ · Σ L_j R_j e_x`. | A 3D chain needs a complete frame per vector; yaw-pitch-roll about the moving axes is one standard choice and reduces to the 2D convention (A12) when pitch = roll = 0. The pitch sign surprises people who expect "positive = up". | Implementer's choice (D37, provisional). Derivations: `tests/core/test_loop3d.py`. |

## GD&T conversions (Phase 4)

Revised 2026-11-13. The maintainer delegated the review ("verify it on your own, just don't falsely verify it"). What was verified, and against what, is stated per row. **ASME Y14.5-2018 itself was not available** (paywalled); only the Rule #1 paragraph number could be confirmed from a public source. Other section numbers are deliberately not given.

| # | Convention | Basis and known weakness | Verification |
|---|---|---|---|
| A17 | **Flatness / parallelism**, zone width t. Default: the tolerance is a refinement *inside* the size limits of the feature of size it sits on and adds **no** variation to a stack through that size (`within_size=True`). Where the envelope rule does not apply (independency, stock, free state, a surface located by profile or a basic dimension, or a datum that is another feature) it is added as a contributor `0 ± t/2` (`within_size=False`). Parallelism has no default: the caller must say which case it is. | Rule #1, the envelope principle: "the form of a regular feature of size is controlled by its limits of size". Limits: a point-to-plane measurement from a datum plane can still exceed the size limits by rocking; and under ISO 8015 (independency by default) the rule does not hold at all — this tool assumes ASME. | Rule #1 = ASME Y14.5-2018 **5.8.1** (2009: 2.7.1), confirmed from GD&T Basics, https://www.gdandtbasics.com/?p=8382 . Geometry argument checked by the reviewer subagent (phase-4 report). |
| A18 | **Position, RFS**, diametral zone T → along one stack direction, a contributor `0 ± T/2`, normal, σ = T/(6·cp). | Maintainer's choice ("normal, 3 sigma"). Consistent in 2D with an isotropic bivariate normal of σ = T/6 per axis; the radial deviation is then Rayleigh and exp(−4.5) = 1.11 % of parts lie outside the cylindrical zone at cp = 1. | Arithmetic: reviewer + `validation/verify_golden.py`. Standard text: not consulted. |
| A19 | **Position at MMC / LMC**: bonus B = departure of the actual size from the MMC size towards LMC (LMC callout: from the LMC size towards MMC), limited to `[0, size range]`. Zone = T + B. Hole: MMC = smallest size, LMC = largest; pin: the opposite. | Bonus = actual feature size − MMC size (public GD&T references). A part beyond its MMC size is a size reject and earns no bonus, hence the lower clamp; the upper clamp follows from the size limits. | Definition from public sources (GD&T Basics; FARO; Xometry — not the standard). Arithmetic: reviewer + verify_golden. |
| A20 | **Two models for the bonus, answering two questions.** (a) *Stack ("zone-fill") model*: `δ = (T + B)/(6·cp)·z`; worst-case half-range `(T + size range)/2`; σ = √E[(T+B)²]/(6·cp). The deviation is allowed to use the enlarged zone, so against a fixed positional limit failures go **up** (3.81e-3 vs 6.8e-6 in the test case). The output is a scale mixture of normals; the normal-theory P_fail is 25 % low there and a `NON_NORMAL_INPUT` warning is raised. (b) *Fixed-process (inspection) model*: positional scatter σ_p = T/(6·cp) does not depend on size; a part is rejected if r > (T + B)/2. Here the bonus only widens acceptance, so the reject rate goes **down** (2.6e-4 vs 1.11e-2). | The two standard stack-up texts (Drake; Fischer) disagree on how to treat the bonus in a statistical stack (reported in an engineering forum thread; the books themselves were not read). detectTOL therefore offers the conservative zone-fill value for stacks and the fixed-process value for inspection, and labels both. Worst case with the bonus added is common to both texts. | Arithmetic: reviewer + verify_golden. Choice of model: implementer's, documented, **not** derived from a standard. |
| A21 | **Perpendicularity / angularity**, zone width t over feature length Lf → angular tolerance `± atan(t/Lf)` rad on a loop vector. | Geometry of a plane inside a zone of width t. Assumes the whole zone is used by tilt (no form error); projected along a vector longer than the feature it gives more than t. | Arithmetic: reviewer + verify_golden. Standard text: not consulted. |
| A22 | **Datums**: primary / secondary / tertiary **planar** datum features only. Labels and precedence are validated; the frame is not used to compute directions — the user supplies each sensitivity. No datum targets, no material modifiers on datums, no common datums in the model (the STEP reader reports them as `A-B`), no non-planar datum features. | Smallest model that lets callouts be checked for consistency. | Scope decision; nothing to verify numerically. |

## STEP input (Phase 5)

| # | Assumption | Reason | Reference |
|---|---|---|---|
| A23 | Lengths from a STEP file are millimetres and angles radians. Geometry comes from OCCT (which converts the file's length unit); PMI values come from the file text and are converted with the file's own unit definitions (SI prefixes, `CONVERSION_BASED_UNIT` chains such as INCH and DEGREE). A unit that cannot be resolved is an error, not a guess. | root.md rule 5. | `tests/cad/test_step_pmi_text.py` |
| A24 | PMI *values* (dimension nominal and signed tolerances, geometric tolerance type, magnitude, MMC/LMC modifier, datum references) are read from the text of the file, not from OCCT's XCAF transfer. XCAF supplies only the link from a PMI item to faces, matched on type, name and value. | XCAF drops 34 of 362 geometric tolerances on the NIST files and returns lower deviations unsigned. The text has all of them. | `docs/reports/phase-5.md`, `tests/cad/test_nist_text.py` |
| A25 | Shapes without any face (wireframe or annotation geometry beside the solid) are not parts and are skipped, with a note. Face index = position in OCCT's `TopExp_Explorer` order within a part; stable for one file, not guaranteed across CAD re-exports, which is why annotation files also carry a centroid/normal fingerprint. | Observed in every NIST file. | `docs/reports/phase-5.md` |
| A26 | A PMI item whose face link OCCT does not provide is still returned, with an empty `targets` and a note on the model. A zero positional tolerance is accepted from the file only with an MMC or LMC modifier. | Nothing is dropped silently. | `tests/cad/test_nist.py` |
